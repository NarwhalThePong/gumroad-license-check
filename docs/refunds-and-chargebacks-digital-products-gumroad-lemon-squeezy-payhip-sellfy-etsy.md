Sooner or later a buyer asks for their money back, or skips asking and goes to their bank. The first is a **refund**: you (or the platform) send the money back. The second is a **chargeback** (also called a dispute): the card company or PayPal pulls the payment back, and someone has to answer it with evidence. On most platforms a refund costs you a few cents to a couple of dollars, while a lost chargeback can cost more than the sale itself.

We read each company's own help pages, fee pages and terms on October 20, 2026. Rules and fees change, so check the linked pages before you rely on them.

## The short version

- **Gumroad** (sales it processes itself): on a refund, Gumroad returns its own 10% + $0.50 fee and keeps only the card processing part (2.9% + $0.30). On a chargeback you cover the refunded amount and the processing fees, Gumroad returns its platform fee, and Gumroad fights the dispute for you. Its help pages don't list a separate dispute fee for sellers.
- **Lemon Squeezy**: a refund comes out of your next payout minus its platform fee, so you're back where you started. A chargeback costs a **$15 dispute fee** on top, and Lemon Squeezy handles the case.
- **Payhip** and **Sellfy** don't hold your money, so you refund and fight disputes yourself in your own **PayPal** or **Stripe** account. PayPal and Stripe keep their original fees on a refund, and both charge **$15** for a dispute (Stripe charges another $15 if you counter it, returned only if you win).
- **Etsy** credits its transaction and processing fees back when you refund through Shop Manager; the $0.20 listing fee comes back only if you also cancel the order. Etsy answers chargebacks for you and can take the money back from your Etsy balance.
- Everywhere, **a refund is far cheaper than a lost chargeback**, and too many disputes can get your account restricted.

## What a refund costs on PayPal and Stripe

If you sell through Payhip, Sellfy or your own website, the processor's rules apply directly to you:

- **Stripe** doesn't return its original processing fee (2.9% + 30¢ on a US card) when you refund. A dispute costs a **$15 dispute received fee**, which Stripe doesn't return even if you win, and a **$15 dispute countered fee** if you respond, which you get back only if you win. Card networks typically let buyers dispute for 120 days after the payment, and while a dispute is open you can't refund outside it.
- **PayPal** charges nothing to make a refund, but the fees you paid to receive the payment (3.49% + $0.49 for PayPal Checkout in the US) aren't returned. Its **standard dispute fee is $15** in the US. PayPal waives it for inquiries that never become a claim, cases you settle with the buyer, and claims the buyer files with PayPal as unauthorized. Sellers with a dispute rate of 1.5% or more and over 100 sales in three months pay a higher fee.

## One $20 sale, refunded or charged back

A $20 digital download bought by a US buyer with a card (PayPal account on the PayPal lines). We leave sales tax out to keep the math simple. "Net" is what you end up with after the sale and the reversal, compared with never having made the sale; the buyer gets the full $20 back in every case.

| Platform and payment | You keep on the sale | Net after a full refund | Net after a lost chargeback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gumroad (Gumroad processes the card) | $16.62 | −$0.88 | −$0.88 (no seller dispute fee listed) |
| Lemon Squeezy | $18.50 | $0.00 | −$15.00 |
| Payhip Free + Stripe | $18.12 | −$0.88, or −$1.88 if Payhip keeps its 5% | −$15.88 to −$16.88; $15 more if you counter and lose |
| Payhip Free + PayPal | $17.81 | −$1.19, or −$2.19 if Payhip keeps its 5% | −$16.19 to −$17.19 |
| Sellfy + Stripe | $19.12 | −$0.88 | −$15.88; −$30.88 if you counter and lose |
| Sellfy + PayPal | $18.81 | −$1.19 | −$16.19 |
| Etsy | $17.65 | $0.00 if you also cancel, −$0.20 if you don't | Not stated on Etsy's pages; −$2.35 if none of its fees come back |

How we got there:
- **Gumroad:** fee 10% + $0.50 = $2.50, processing 2.9% + $0.30 = $0.88, so you keep $16.62. A refund returns the $2.50, so you're out the $0.88 processing. Gumroad's chargeback page says you cover the refunded amount and processing fees and Gumroad returns its platform fee, so a lost chargeback lands in the same place.
- **Lemon Squeezy:** platform fee 5% + $0.50 = $1.50 on a US card, so you keep $18.50. A refund takes $20 minus the $1.50 fee ($18.50) from your next payout; a chargeback takes that plus $15.
- **Payhip Free:** Payhip's 5% = $1.00, plus Stripe $0.88 (you keep $18.12) or PayPal 3.49% + $0.49 = $1.19 (you keep $17.81). On a refund the processor keeps its fee. Payhip doesn't say whether its own fee comes back, so we show both cases. A lost dispute adds the $15 fee.
- **Sellfy:** no Sellfy transaction fee (you pay its monthly plan instead), so only the processor's fee and the $15 dispute fee count. On Stripe, countering and losing adds the $15 countered fee; even a won Stripe dispute costs the $15 received fee.
- **Etsy:** $0.20 listing fee, 6.5% transaction fee ($1.30) and 3% + $0.25 processing ($0.85), so you keep $17.65. A Shop Manager refund credits the transaction and processing fees, and the listing fee too if you cancel.

The pattern: if a buyer asks for a refund on a $20 download, giving it back costs you at most a couple of dollars. Letting it turn into a chargeback costs about $15 on Lemon Squeezy, Payhip and Sellfy, whether or not the buyer was right.

## How to keep chargebacks down

These come from the platforms' own advice:
- **Answer refund requests quickly.** Gumroad notes that some buyers open chargebacks simply because they can't reach the seller, and Sellfy says fast replies often settle a problem before it becomes a refund request or a chargeback.
- **State your refund policy on the product page.** Gumroad includes the policy you set in any card or PayPal dispute; Sellfy suggests stating a no-refunds policy clearly if you have one. A no-refunds policy doesn't stop chargebacks: card networks still allow them.
- **Describe exactly what the buyer gets** (file type, pages, license terms) so it can't feel "not as described".
- **Use a recognizable name.** Gumroad's statement descriptor starts with "gumrd.com" followed by your name or product, so use your brand as your full name if that's what buyers know.
- **Keep the evidence.** Payhip shows the download date, time, browser and IP address for each customer, and Sellfy keeps an Activity log for each order; both suggest screenshots for disputes.
- **Refund, don't fight, when the buyer has a point.** And when Stripe flags a payment with an early fraud warning, its own analysis says refunding pays off on charges about the size of your dispute fee or smaller.

## Best for

- **Gumroad:** sellers who'd rather not handle disputes; Gumroad fights them and doesn't list a dispute fee for its own sales.
- **Lemon Squeezy:** sellers who want the platform to deal with chargebacks and accept a $15 fee when one happens.
- **Payhip and Sellfy:** sellers who want their money straight in PayPal or Stripe and are happy to manage refunds and disputes there.
- **Etsy:** marketplace sellers who refund now and then; the fees come back when you refund through Shop Manager.

## Downsides

- **Gumroad:** the highest fee on direct sales, and the card processing part isn't returned on a refund. Gumroad can refund buyers on your behalf within 90 days, and a high dispute rate brings an enforced refund policy or a 25% reserve.
- **Lemon Squeezy:** $15 per chargeback, and it can refund buyers within 60 days even if you have a no-refunds policy.
- **Payhip:** you handle every refund and dispute yourself, and its pages don't say whether its fee comes back on a refund.
- **Sellfy:** the same, and Sellfy support can't help a buyer with a refund or step into a dispute.
- **Etsy:** the buyer's bank or PayPal, not Etsy, decides a chargeback, and Etsy can take the money back from your account; when Etsy itself covers a refund under its Purchase Protection program, your fees aren't credited back. Its Purchase Protection rules are written around shipped orders (shipping times, tracking), so don't count on them for downloads.
