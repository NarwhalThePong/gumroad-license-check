**Not tax advice.** This guide sums up what each platform says on its own help pages and what a few tax authorities publish. Your situation depends on where you live and where your buyers are, so check with a tax professional before you rely on it.

Sell one PDF to a buyer in Germany and, in principle, German VAT is due on it, even if you live in Ohio. The question is who has to charge it and send it to the tax office: you, or the platform you sell through. Some platforms take the whole job off your hands as **merchant of record** (they are the seller in the eyes of the tax office). Others pay the money straight into your own PayPal or Stripe account and leave the tax with you.

We read each company's own help pages and terms on October 20, 2026. Tax rules change often, so check the linked pages before you rely on them.

## The short version

- **Gumroad** and **Lemon Squeezy** are merchants of record for your sales. They charge the tax at checkout, keep it and pay it to the tax offices. You don't charge, file or pay sales tax or VAT on those sales yourself.
- **Payhip** pays you through your own PayPal or Stripe account, but it takes on **digital EU and UK VAT** (as your reseller) and **US and Canadian sales tax** (as a marketplace facilitator in the US, since July 1, 2026, and a digital platform operator in Canada). Taxes in other countries stay with you.
- **Sellfy** is not a merchant of record. It can add EU VAT and the rates you set at checkout, but the tax lands in your account and filing and paying it is your job.
- **Etsy** keeps you as the seller but collects and pays US sales tax as a marketplace facilitator in 46 states plus DC and Puerto Rico, and VAT or GST on digital downloads to buyers in the EU, the UK and 39 other countries on its list.
- On every platform, **income tax** on what you earn is still yours.

A few details from the same pages:

- **Gumroad** always adds VAT on top of your price. Its EU and UK VAT page answers "Can I choose not to charge VAT on my digital products? Or make it inclusive?" with "No." Buyers with a VAT ID can get the VAT refunded, and a refund to the buyer includes the VAT. You can mark a product as an e-publication to get reduced VAT rates (0% in the UK).
- **Lemon Squeezy** deducts the tax from your next payout and pays it. You can turn on tax-inclusive pricing for the whole store, in which case the tax comes out of your price instead of being added to it.
- **Payhip** handles EU and UK VAT by default, and you can switch that off if you'd rather do it yourself. It charges nothing extra for US sales tax. You choose whether tax is added on top of your price or included in it.
- **Sellfy** applies current EU VAT rates automatically once you enable tax. You add the UK and any US states yourself. You can block buyers from the EU and the UK so you don't have to register there (buyers in your own country can still buy).
- **Etsy** adds digital VAT to the listing price automatically and, with Etsy Payments, deducts it before the money reaches your Payment account. It doesn't charge anything extra for collecting US sales tax.

## What happens if the platform doesn't collect

If you sell on Sellfy, or into a country Payhip doesn't cover, these are the rules you run into:

- **EU:** VAT on electronic services sold to consumers (downloads count) is always charged at the rate of the buyer's EU country. A business outside the EU can register for the EU's **non-Union One Stop Shop** in one EU country and file one VAT return each quarter for all its EU sales. The EUR 10,000 threshold you may have read about is for businesses based in the EU.
- **UK:** digital services sold to UK consumers are liable to UK VAT, and HMRC says a business based outside the UK "will need to register for UK VAT". HMRC adds that when you sell through a digital platform or marketplace that sets the general terms, authorises payment or handles delivery or download, the platform is responsible for the VAT instead of you; Sellfy's own help still tells its sellers to collect, register and report UK VAT themselves.
- **US states:** each state sets its own rules. Pennsylvania, for example, charges its 6% sales tax on digital products such as e-books, apps and photos, but a seller from another state only has to register and collect once its Pennsylvania sales top **$100,000** in a calendar year. Below that, the buyer is supposed to pay use tax themselves.

## One $20 sale, three buyers

Say you live in the US and sell a $20 downloadable template (not an e-book, so standard rates apply) to one buyer in **Germany** (19% VAT), one in the **UK** (20% VAT) and one in **Pennsylvania** (6% state sales tax). Each platform adds the tax on top of the $20 price, the buyer pays by card, and you're on Payhip's free plan. Your Pennsylvania sales are far below $100,000.

What the buyer pays:

| Buyer | Tax | Buyer pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Germany | 19% of $20 = $3.80 | $23.80 |
| UK | 20% of $20 = $4.00 | $24.00 |
| Pennsylvania | 6% of $20 = $1.20 | $21.20 |

What you keep from each sale, after the tax and the platform's own fees:

| Platform | Germany | UK | Pennsylvania | Who pays the tax |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gumroad (direct sale) | $16.62 | $16.62 | $16.62 | Gumroad |
| Lemon Squeezy | $17.95 | $17.94 | $18.44 | Lemon Squeezy |
| Payhip Free (before PayPal/Stripe fees) | $19.00 | $19.00 | $19.00 | Payhip |
| Sellfy (before PayPal/Stripe fees and the monthly plan) | $20.00, plus $3.80 VAT you must pay | $20.00, plus $4.00 VAT you must pay | $20.00, no tax charged | You (Germany, UK); in Pennsylvania nobody collects and the buyer owes use tax |
| Etsy | $17.54 | $17.53 | $17.61 | Etsy |

How each line works:

- **Gumroad:** 10% + $0.50 = $2.50, plus card processing of 2.9% + $0.30 = $0.88, leaves $16.62. The tax goes to Gumroad on top of that. We worked both fees out on the $20 price; Gumroad's help doesn't say whether card processing is figured on the tax-inclusive total, which would cost you up to 12 cents more.
- **Lemon Squeezy:** the fee is $0.50 + 5% of the **total including tax**, plus 1.5% for buyers outside the US. Germany: $0.50 + $1.19 + $0.36 = $2.05, so $23.80 − $3.80 − $2.05 = $17.95. UK: $0.50 + $1.20 + $0.36 = $2.06, leaving $17.94 (the same method as Lemon Squeezy's own $15 UK example: $0.50 + 5% of the $18 total + 1.5% = $1.67). Pennsylvania: $0.50 + $1.06 = $1.56, leaving $18.44.
- **Payhip Free:** Payhip's VAT example takes its 5% on the price before tax, so 5% of $20 = $1.00, and Payhip keeps the tax. You get $19.00 in your PayPal or Stripe account, and PayPal or Stripe then takes its own fee. On Plus (2%) you'd get $19.60, on Pro (0%) $20.00. We assumed the same 5% base for US sales tax.
- **Sellfy:** Sellfy takes no transaction fee (you pay a monthly plan instead), and the whole amount the buyer pays goes to your PayPal or Stripe account: $23.80 from Germany and $24.00 from the UK, if you've enabled EU VAT and added the UK. The $3.80 and $4.00 aren't yours: you need EU and UK VAT registrations to pay them. In Pennsylvania you're under the $100,000 threshold, so you don't charge tax and keep $20.00. Sellfy's pricing page puts PayPal or Stripe fees at "typically 2.9% + 30¢".
- **Etsy (US shop, US bank account):** 6.5% transaction fee on the $20 item ($1.30), $0.20 to renew the listing after the sale, and payment processing of 3% + $0.25 on the total including tax. Germany: 3% of $23.80 + $0.25 = $0.96, so $23.80 − $3.80 − $1.30 − $0.96 − $0.20 = $17.54. UK: $0.97, leaving $17.53. Pennsylvania: $0.89, leaving $17.61. Etsy's page says processing includes "any applicable sales tax"; we applied that to VAT too.
- **Pennsylvania on Gumroad and Lemon Squeezy:** neither publishes a state-by-state list. We assumed both charge the 6%, since Pennsylvania taxes digital products and both say they handle sales tax as merchant of record. If they don't, the buyer pays $20.00 and you keep the same $16.62 on Gumroad and $18.50 on Lemon Squeezy (its fee is a little lower without tax in the total).

Payout fees (for example Gumroad's 2% on PayPal payouts) aren't included. See [When do you get paid?](when-do-you-get-paid-gumroad-payhip-etsy-lemon-squeezy-sellfy) for those.

What the table shows: the platforms that keep the most per sale (Sellfy, then Payhip) are the ones that hand you more of the tax work, or a monthly bill. On Sellfy, every EU or UK sale means VAT registrations and quarterly returns, which is a lot of paperwork for a $20 product. Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy and Etsy cost more per sale, but you have nothing to file for the places they cover.

## Best for

- **Gumroad:** sellers who want no tax work at all, anywhere, and no monthly fee. The same fee applies wherever the buyer lives.
- **Lemon Squeezy:** software and digital product sellers who want a merchant of record with a lower percentage than Gumroad's 10%, especially for US buyers.
- **Payhip:** sellers who want a free plan and low fees and mostly sell to the EU, UK, US and Canada, where Payhip handles the tax.
- **Sellfy:** sellers who already have a tax setup (or an accountant) and want to be the seller of record with no transaction fees, or who sell only in their own country.
- **Etsy:** sellers who want marketplace buyers; US sales tax and digital VAT in most big markets are handled.

## Downsides

- **Gumroad:** the highest fee here on direct sales (10% + $0.50 plus card processing), and you can't make prices tax-inclusive.
- **Lemon Squeezy:** extra 1.5% on international and PayPal payments, and the fee is figured on the total including tax.
- **Payhip:** buyers outside the EU, UK, US and Canada (Australia, Norway or Switzerland, for example) are your responsibility if tax applies there. US sales tax handling only started on July 1, 2026.
- **Sellfy:** all the tax work is yours. Selling to the EU or UK means registering there, or blocking those buyers.
- **Etsy:** several fees per sale, processing is charged on tax you never keep, and you can't opt out of Etsy's tax collection.
